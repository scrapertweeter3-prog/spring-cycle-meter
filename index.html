The garage door spring is a meter, not a part

A garage door gets judged by its panels and its motor, and the torsion spring above the door gets judged by nobody, which is backwards, because the spring is the part doing the actual lifting. Springs are sold on a cycle rating, and one cycle is one open plus one close. A two car household that comes and goes four times a day runs 4 x 365 = 1,460 cycles a year through a piece of hardware most owners never think about until it snaps.

Builder grade springs carry a 10,000 cycle rating. A replacement visit, spring plus labor, runs about $250, so $250 / 10,000 cycles = $0.025 a cycle. The high cycle upgrade on the same visit swaps in a 50,000 cycle spring for roughly $100 more, so $350 / 50,000 cycles = $0.007 a cycle. Priced by the decade at 14,600 cycles, the builder spring bills $0.025 x 14,600 = $365 a decade and the high cycle spring bills $0.007 x 14,600 = $102.20.

A 100,000 cycle spring runs about $70 over the 50,000, $420 all in, and $420 / 100,000 cycles = $0.0042 a cycle, which is $0.0042 x 14,600 = $61.32 a decade. At 1,460 cycles a year the 50,000 spring is good for 34.2 years and the 100,000 spring covers 68.5 years of comings and goings. Builders still hang the cheap wire because they size for average use, 3 cycles a day, 3 x 365 = 1,095 a year, which stretches a 10,000 cycle spring to 9.1 years, past the typical first owner and onto somebody else's invoice.

The cycle rating is also the warranty, written in wire gauge instead of sentences. A spring that snaps at 8,000 of its 10,000 rated cycles is not defective, it is on schedule, and the manufacturer priced it exactly that way. The parts covered by sentence warranties, panels and opener, are the parts that barely move. The one component wearing on a clock is the one sold by arithmetic.

Failure day adds its own surcharge. A broken spring leaves the door half open, a 300 pound panel becomes a two person lift, and the same day service call runs $75 to $150 over the scheduled rate. The high cycle spring costs $100 more once and never collects that premium. That is the entire trade, $100 one time against a per decade bill three and a half times larger.

Garage door math like this runs on DurablePicks, cost per decade tables for the rest of the house at durablepicks.com.

The spring was counting the whole time.
